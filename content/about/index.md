I’m Mehdi Hasanzadeh Samadi—usually Farokh—a DevOps engineer based in Tehran. I build and operate the infrastructure, automation, and delivery systems that help software run reliably in production.

My background began close to the operating system: Linux administration, networking, storage, virtualization, and practical troubleshooting. That foundation still shapes how I approach modern platforms—I want to understand what is happening below the abstraction, not only how to operate the abstraction itself.

My professional path includes DevOps engineering at **Irancell Labs**, **Asiatech**, and **Atieh Dadeh Pardaz**, along with earlier work in Linux systems administration and solution architecture.

## Systems foundation

Linux is the center of my systems background. I work across compute, networking, storage, and virtualized environments, with an emphasis on understanding failure modes and making operational procedures explicit.

## Automation and infrastructure as code

I use **Bash**, **Python**, and **Golang** to remove repetitive work and build operational tooling. **Ansible** and **Terraform** turn system configuration and infrastructure provisioning into reviewable, repeatable processes.

## Containers, platforms, and delivery

My platform work spans containers, **Docker**, **Kubernetes**, and **Helm**, together with the CI/CD systems that move changes safely toward production. I work with delivery tooling including **Jenkins** and focus on pipelines that are understandable, traceable, and recoverable.

## Operating with feedback

Reliable systems need useful signals. I use systems including **Prometheus** and **Elasticsearch** to make behavior visible through metrics and logs, shorten diagnosis, and improve platforms using what production teaches us.

## How I approach systems

- Automate repetitive work without hiding important decisions
- Prefer observable systems over operational guesswork
- Make dangerous procedures explicit, validated, and reversible
- Treat networking, storage, and operating-system behavior as part of the platform
- Keep infrastructure understandable as it grows
- Share tools and lessons that can save someone else time

One example is my interactive Bash utility for [safer Kubernetes node upgrades](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/interactive-bash-script-safe-custom-kubernetes-node-mehdi-frzzf). It detects node roles, guides component selection, validates requested versions, and handles the differing needs of worker and control-plane nodes.
